Antideploy takes any application you've built — whether with Claude Code, Cursor, Codex, a low-code tool, or classic hand-written code — and handles everything that comes after: analyzing your codebase, provisioning infrastructure, running your build pipeline, deploying to production, and keeping it healthy over time. You write (or generate) the code. Antideploy handles everything else.

## What Antideploy Is

Antideploy is a **deployment and operations platform**, not an app builder. You bring your own application from whatever tool or workflow you already use, and Antideploy figures out what it needs to run — then provisions it automatically.

When you connect a repository or upload your code, Antideploy reads your project and determines:

- **Language and runtime** — Node.js, Python, Go, Ruby, PHP, and more
- **Framework** — Next.js, Django, Laravel, FastAPI, Express, Rails, and dozens of others
- **Database requirements** — detected from ORM configuration, connection strings, or migration files
- **Background workers, queues, and cron jobs** — inferred from job definitions and scheduler config
- **Static assets and media storage** — detected from asset pipeline and upload code

It then provisions exactly the infrastructure your app requires, with no YAML, no Terraform, no cloud console configuration.

## What You Never Configure Manually

Antideploy automatically handles everything in this list. You never touch it unless you want to override a default:

- **Compute** — instance sizing, auto-scaling, health checks, and restarts
- **PostgreSQL** — provisioned, connected, and backed up automatically when detected
- **Object storage** — provisioned for file uploads, media, and static assets
- **SSL certificates** — issued and renewed automatically for every deployment
- **Networking** — internal service mesh, load balancing, and edge routing
- **Secrets management** — encrypted environment variable storage with injection at runtime
- **Background queues and workers** — provisioned from your job definitions
- **Cron jobs** — scheduled from your existing cron or task-scheduler config
- **Build pipeline** — detected and executed without a CI configuration file
- **Monitoring and alerting** — activated automatically after your first successful deploy

## The Full Deployment Lifecycle

Antideploy isn't just a deploy button — it manages your application continuously:

1. **Analyze** — reads your code to understand what your app is and what it needs
2. **Provision** — creates exactly the infrastructure required, nothing more
3. **Build** — compiles, bundles, and prepares your application for production
4. **Deploy** — rolls out your app with zero-downtime deployments by default
5. **Monitor** — tracks uptime, performance, and errors from the moment you go live
6. **Scale** — adjusts compute resources automatically under load
7. **Diagnose** — surfaces issues in plain language via the App Intelligence layer

<Tip>
  The App Intelligence layer understands your app's specific stack and context. When something goes wrong, it doesn't just show you raw logs — it explains what happened and suggests fixes in terms of your actual code.
</Tip>
